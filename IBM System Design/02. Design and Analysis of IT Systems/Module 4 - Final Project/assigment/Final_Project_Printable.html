Final Project: Digital Transformation Strategy for Retailer

Course: Design & Analysis of IT Systems Role: Lead Systems Analyst Target: Unified Cloud Commerce (50 → 100+ Stores)

Step 1: Assess Current Systems (9 Points)

1.1 Summary of Current System Architecture

The retailer currently operates an outdated, fragmented infrastructure across approximately 50 physical stores and an independent online shop:

  • Point-of-Sale (POS) Systems: In-store terminals run on legacy Windows CE operating systems with outdated card reader drivers from 2009. Transaction checkout latency regularly exceeds 5 seconds.
  • E-Commerce Platform: Built on Magento 1.9 backed by an isolated MySQL database. Performance is severely degraded with Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS > 0.25) and initial page load exceeding 2 seconds on 4G networks.
  • Data Storage & Architecture: Each store hosts a separate on-premise SQL database. Transactions synchronize to Headquarters (HQ) via nightly batch ETL jobs, resulting in fragmented data silos and out-of-sync customer records.
  • Inventory Management: Relies entirely on manual data entry via Excel/CSV spreadsheets, causing high stock visibility latency, inventory inaccuracies, and delayed supplier reordering.

1.2 Pain-Point Matrix (Issues, Impacts, and Root Causes)

Operational Area Specific Pain Point / Issue Business & Customer Impact Technical Root Cause
Checkout (In-store & Online) High latency (>5s POS checkout, >2s 4G web load, CLS > 0.25). Cart abandonment, reduced conversion rates, long queues, and customer churn. Obsolete Windows CE hardware, 2009 peripheral drivers, and unoptimized monolithic Magento 1.9 architecture.
Restocking & Inventory Frequent stockouts, overstocks, and supplier reorder delays. Lost sales revenue, inability to fulfill orders, and tied-up working capital. Disconnected manual Excel spreadsheets and lack of automated real-time inventory decrement across channels.
Marketing & CRM Split customer lists and disconnected purchasing history. Inability to run personalized omni-channel campaigns or loyalty programs. Siloed store-level SQL databases updating only via nightly batch jobs to HQ with no centralized Customer Master Data.

1.3 Key Inefficiencies Restricting Scaling & Agility

  1. Manual CSV Imports & Duplicate Data Entry: Operations depend on spreadsheet reconciliation, causing high overhead and error risks.
  2. Inconsistent Customer Data across Channels: Absence of a Single Customer View (SCV) impedes marketing personalization.
  3. Sluggish POS and Web Interfaces: Degraded response times lower customer satisfaction and limit transaction volume.
  4. Lack of Real-Time Inventory Visibility: Obstructs Buy Online, Pickup In-Store (BOPIS) and blocks scalable expansion to 100+ stores.

Step 2: Define Stakeholder Requirements (10 Points)

2.1 Stakeholders and Their Interests

  • Customers: Demand frictionless checkout, sub-2s page load, unified shopping cart, and 100% accurate real-time inventory visibility.
  • Store Staff: Need reliable, intuitive POS terminals (≤5s transactions) with multi-store inventory lookup for quick customer service.
  • Management: Seek centralized data intelligence, 360° customer profile, real-time margin analytics, and seamless scaling to 100+ stores.
  • IT Team: Prioritize low maintenance overhead, modern API-first cloud architecture, and strict security compliance (PCI-DSS, GDPR).

2.2 Functional Requirements (FR) & Justifications

ID Functional Requirement Specification Detailed Justification & Pain Point Addressed
FR-01 Process in-store sales transactions at POS within ≤ 5 seconds (including payment gateway authorization). Eliminates counter queue congestion and directly replaces obsolete Windows CE hardware/driver bottlenecks.
FR-02 Automatic real-time inventory decrement across all sales channels upon transaction commit. Prevents overselling, eliminates manual Excel updates, and resolves chronic store stockouts.
FR-03 Provide a 360° Customer Profile API consolidating in-store & online purchasing history and loyalty. Eliminates batch sync silos; empowers marketing to execute personalized omnichannel campaigns.
FR-04 Support Buy Online, Pickup In-Store (BOPIS) and cross-channel inventory reservation workflows. Meets modern consumer expectations and delivers seamless omnichannel fulfillment capabilities.

2.3 Non-Functional Requirements (NFR) & Justifications

ID Non-Functional Requirement Specification Detailed Justification & Pain Point Addressed
NFR-01 System Availability: High Availability ≥ 99.9% uptime (24x7x365) with automated cloud failover. Ensures uninterrupted revenue during peak business hours and mission-critical retail continuity.
NFR-02 Performance / Latency: E-commerce page load < 2s on 4G networks with CLS ≤ 0.1. Prevents customer drop-off, improves mobile conversion rates, and maximizes search engine ranking.
NFR-03 Scalability & Elasticity: Elastic scaling to support 3x holiday peak traffic and 100+ stores. Future-proofs the technical infrastructure to sustain corporate expansion without re-architecting.

3.2 Recommendation and Rationale

Recommended Solution: Option A - Salesforce Commerce Cloud + POS (Tableau Retail)

Strategic Rationale:

  • Ideal for 100+ Store Expansion: Enterprise-grade scalability (10/10) with native omnichannel Order Management (OMS), unified customer CRM, and Tableau Retail analytics.
  • Timeline Alignment: 6-month deployment time-to-value fits well inside management's strict 9-month window prior to the holiday season.
  • Rejection Rationale: Shopify Plus (Option B) suffers from multi-store scalability limitations beyond 100 enterprise stores; Custom Microservices (Option C) requires 12 months (exceeding timeline), costs $300k, and carries high execution risks.

3.3 Trade-Offs for the Recommended Solution

  • Cost vs. Scalability: Year 1 investment ($150,000) is higher than Shopify Plus ($50,000), but eliminates future migration costs when expanding to 100+ stores.
  • Customization vs. Delivery Speed: Uses standard enterprise workflows rather than bespoke code, significantly reducing technical debt and long-term maintenance burdens.

Step 4: Feasibility and Risk Analysis (8 Points)

4.1 Technical, Economic, and Operational Feasibility

  • Technical Feasibility: Fully feasible. Cloud SaaS platform provides robust RESTful APIs, zero store-level database footprint, and certified hardware integrations.
  • Economic Feasibility: Strong positive Net Present Value (NPV) over 5 years. Reduced maintenance and eliminated stockouts offset the $150k initial investment.
  • Operational Feasibility: Highly feasible. Intuitive interfaces enable comprehensive staff upskilling within 8 weeks without disrupting ongoing retail sales.
